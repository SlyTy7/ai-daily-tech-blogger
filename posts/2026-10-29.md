Node.js 26 just became LTS, and the release notes are almost comically short. [Node.js 26.13.0 'Lithium'](https://nodejs.org/en/blog/release/v26.13.0) contains, in the project's own words, no changes from 26.12.1 other than metadata such as the `process.release` object. It will get updates through the end of April 2029.

My position: that's exactly how an LTS promotion should look, and you should still not rush your production services onto it today.

## Why the empty release is good news

An LTS release is a label, not a feature drop. Node 26.13.0 is the same code that's been running as "Current" since 26.0, now with a support promise attached. If you've been testing against 26.x, there's nothing new to re-test. The promotion didn't sneak in a late behavior change, which is the failure mode I'd actually worry about.

It's also a useful reminder of what "Current" means in practice. Node 26.12.0, which we covered a few days ago, was already the thing LTS would ship. Anyone who wanted to evaluate 26 had weeks of runway to do it on a version that wasn't going to move under them.

## Why I'd still wait a bit

Here's the strongest case for waiting, and I think it holds up:

- **Your ecosystem lags the runtime.** Native addons, build tooling, and APM agents tend to certify against LTS, not Current. The LTS label is often the trigger for vendors to officially support a version, so the first weeks are when you find out which dependency isn't ready.
- **Node 24 is still actively maintained.** [Node.js 24.22.2 (LTS)](https://nodejs.org/en/blog/release/v24.22.2) shipped the same day, with V8 backports and a fix to `stream` so it keeps the async context of parked writes. A line that's still getting care is a perfectly good place to sit.
- **There's a real packaging change.** The 26.13.0 notes point out that since Node 25 the official Linux binaries link against libatomic, which you may need to install separately. If you're coming from 24 or 22, that bites in minimal container images, and you'd rather find out in CI than at 2 a.m.

None of that is a knock on 26. It's just that "now LTS" and "now safe for my stack" are different claims, and only the first one is the Node team's to make.

## The counterpoint

The best argument against my caution is that waiting has a cost too. LTS lines have a finite window, and teams that put off upgrades tend to batch them, which means one painful jump over two majors instead of two easy ones. If your test suite is solid, moving early on a non-critical service is cheap, and it surfaces incompatibilities while you still have time to react. I buy that for a team with good CI. I don't buy it for a team that only learns about breakage from production.

## What I'd actually do

1. Bump the CI matrix to include 26 now, if it isn't there already. It's free signal.
2. Build your container image on 26 and check for the libatomic issue explicitly.
3. Move one low-risk service, such as an internal tool or a webhook consumer, and let it soak.
4. Plan the rest of the fleet for after your dependencies have publicly certified 26.

A calm, boring LTS promotion is the system working. The right response is a calm, boring migration plan, not a flag day.
